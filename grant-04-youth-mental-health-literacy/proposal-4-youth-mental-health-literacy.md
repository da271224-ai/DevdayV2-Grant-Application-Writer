## 1. Statement of Goals

Give Miami Days will help approximately 200 Miami-Dade adolescents build mental-health knowledge, practice safe help-seeking, and identify supportive adults and services. Over 12 months, at least 150 participants will complete four or more learning sessions, and at least 75% of completers will improve on one or more measures of mental-health literacy or confidence seeking help. The program will provide referral information and qualified-provider navigation when additional support is requested.

## 2. Implementation

Give Miami Days proposes a 12-month mental-health literacy and early-support program for approximately 200 middle- and high-school students at partner schools or community sites in Miami-Dade County. The program will offer structured youth groups, caregiver information sessions, and access to qualified professionals for resource navigation or referral guidance. Activities will focus on education, skill practice, and connection to help; they will not diagnose or treat participants.

**Months 1–2: Partner and safety planning.** Confirm participating sites, age groups, family notification and consent requirements, accessible meeting arrangements, and designated school or community referral contacts. Engage a qualified mental-health professional to review the curriculum and advise on referral boundaries, privacy, and urgent safety procedures. Identify local services and verify that referral information is current.

**Months 2–3: Outreach and baseline.** Recruit participants through partner referrals and family outreach. Explain program scope, confidentiality limits, participation expectations, and how urgent concerns will be handled. Collect a brief baseline measure of mental-health literacy and help-seeking confidence using a tool selected with professional and partner input.

**Months 3–10: Youth sessions and caregiver engagement.** Provide six-session youth groups covering emotional awareness, stress and coping, supportive routines, peer-support boundaries, stigma and help-seeking, and how to connect with trusted adults. Run periodic caregiver information sessions on listening, recognizing when support may be needed, and navigating services. Offer professional resource-navigation sessions or referrals according to provider capacity and family consent.

**Months 11–12: Follow-up and improvement.** Administer closeout measures, collect youth and caregiver feedback, review attendance and referrals, and meet with partners to identify access gaps. Produce an aggregate results report and recommend curriculum or delivery changes for the next cycle.

Facilitators will not provide clinical care unless separately qualified and contracted to do so. Disclosures or urgent concerns will be addressed through the agreed school/community safeguarding protocol and applicable law.

### Activities Measures

1. **Youth learning groups:** Enroll approximately 200 adolescents and offer six-session groups; target at least 150 completing four or more sessions.
2. **Caregiver information:** Provide at least four caregiver information sessions during the year, reaching approximately 80 caregivers.
3. **Resource navigation:** Make current referral information available to all participants and offer navigation or referral follow-up to participants or caregivers requesting it.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The project will serve approximately 200 middle- and high-school students in Miami-Dade County who want to strengthen mental-health knowledge and learn how to seek support. Recruitment will take place through participating schools, youth-serving community organizations, and caregiver communication channels. Participation will be voluntary and follow each site’s requirements for family notification, consent, and student assent. A diagnosis will not be required.

Give Miami Days will explain in plain language that the program provides education and resource navigation, not therapy or crisis services. Outreach materials will describe session topics, facilitator roles, privacy limits, and how a young person can speak with a trusted adult. The organization will consult youth and caregivers when selecting examples, session times, and formats. It will ask sites about language and accessibility needs and provide translated materials or interpretation where qualified resources are available.

The program will offer sessions at familiar school or community locations and seek schedules that accommodate students and caregivers. Youth feedback will be collected in age-appropriate ways, including anonymous options. Participants may decline a group activity or leave the program. Staff will provide clear referral contacts and follow partner safety procedures for concerns that require more support. Participation and feedback will be reviewed across sites and age groups, protecting identities, to identify groups who are not being reached or who find the program inaccessible.

## 4. Assessment

The intended outcomes are improved adolescent understanding of mental health, increased confidence identifying a trusted adult or service, and stronger caregiver knowledge of how to start a supportive conversation. Participants will complete a brief literacy and help-seeking measure at enrollment and closeout; the same questions will be used at both points. Staff will track enrollment, attendance, caregiver participation, requests for resource navigation, and referral follow-up where participants consent. A qualified professional will review measures and procedures for age appropriateness and safety.

The program aims to enroll approximately 200 adolescents, have at least 150 complete four or more sessions, and have at least 75% of completers improve on one or more selected measures. At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents should report greater confidence in identifying a next step for seeking support. All participants will receive current referral information; for participants who request navigation, the team will record whether a connection step was completed when follow-up is permitted. Staff will review progress quarterly and adjust schedules or materials with partner input. Results will be reported in aggregate with response rates and limitations. The project will not claim to treat mental illness or attribute clinical outcomes to education sessions.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Youth completion:** At least 150 of approximately 200 enrolled youth complete four or more of six sessions. Measure through attendance records.
2. **Youth literacy and confidence:** At least 75% of completers improve on one or more selected measures. Measure with identical baseline and closeout questions.
3. **Caregiver confidence:** At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents report increased confidence identifying a next step. Measure with an anonymous closeout survey and report response rate.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Give Miami Days will provide a program coordinator to manage site agreements, enrollment, youth and caregiver communication, scheduling, attendance, referrals, and reporting. A qualified mental-health professional will review learning content and advise on referral and safety procedures. Partner schools or youth organizations will provide site contacts and help ensure alignment with existing student-support protocols. Before launch, Give Miami Days will document staff screening and training, privacy and consent practices, insurance, safeguarding procedures, and partner responsibilities.

**Organization-specific qualifications must be verified before submission:** Add the organization’s mission, youth-program experience, current service reach, staff credentials, prior results, and confirmed school, community, and professional partners. Do not imply that clinical or crisis services are available unless a qualified provider has agreed to deliver them. If the organization is new to this work, identify the partner responsible for curriculum review and facilitator preparation.

To sustain the work, the organization will track cost per participant, attendance, youth and caregiver feedback, and referral-navigation demand. It will use findings to refine the curriculum and approach schools, individual donors, employers, and foundations for future support. Reusable materials and partner training can reduce repeated development costs, while continued delivery will depend on qualified professional input, site participation, and secured funding. The organization will not promise scale-up before confirming staffing and service capacity.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Young people may encounter stigma, language barriers, limited access to trusted adults, or uncertainty about where to seek help. The program will be free to participants and will not require a diagnosis. Youth and caregivers will be invited to shape goals, examples, and session formats. Facilitators will use affirming, age-appropriate language and will not assume that every family understands mental-health terms or has the same preferred source of support.

Give Miami Days will work through familiar schools and community organizations, ask about language and disability access, and seek to provide translated materials and flexible scheduling where resources permit. Participants will receive multiple help-seeking options, including trusted adults and community services, rather than a single prescribed route. Staff will explain privacy limits and safety procedures before activities begin. Enrollment, completion, and feedback will be reviewed by site and age group, and by voluntarily provided demographic information when safe to report. The organization will consult families and partners if some youth are less likely to enroll or complete sessions. Priority populations will be defined with local input, not presumed from countywide data.
