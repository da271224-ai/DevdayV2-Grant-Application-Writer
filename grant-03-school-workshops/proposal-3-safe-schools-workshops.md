## 1. Statement of Goals

Give Miami Days will help students and caregivers at up to eight Miami-Dade schools build practical knowledge of emotional safety, stress management, and how to seek help. During one school year, the program will reach approximately 400 students and 80 caregivers; at least 80% of student participants completing the workshops will correctly identify two trusted help-seeking options on a post-session check.

## 2. Implementation

Give Miami Days proposes a 10-month school-based workshop program that complements, rather than replaces, each school’s existing student-support and safety procedures. The program will work with up to eight schools to deliver age-appropriate lessons on recognizing stress, emotional safety, self-regulation, peer-support boundaries, and ways to seek help from a trusted adult. The content will be reviewed with partner schools before delivery. Workshops will not diagnose students, investigate disclosures, or substitute for counseling or crisis response.

**Months 1–2: School agreements and adaptation.** Confirm participating schools, grade bands, staff contacts, parent-notification and consent requirements, accessibility, languages, and the school’s procedures for disclosures or urgent concerns. Review lesson materials with school staff and align referral information with the school’s designated support contacts.

**Months 3–4: Preparation and baseline.** Train facilitators on age-appropriate delivery, privacy limits, safeguarding, referral boundaries, and school-specific escalation steps. Collect brief baseline knowledge checks where the school and families approve. Schedule student and caregiver sessions with the school contact.

**Months 4–9: Workshops and family engagement.** Deliver four student workshops at each participating school. Each school may adapt examples and scheduling for its grade levels while retaining common learning objectives. Offer at least two caregiver workshops per school year across the participating sites, with practical guidance on listening, recognizing concerns, and contacting school or community resources. Provide take-home materials and referral information.

**Months 9–10: Review and reporting.** Collect post-session checks, attendance, and school and caregiver feedback. Meet with each school contact to review fit, referral clarity, participation barriers, and recommended revisions. Submit an aggregate results report to the organization and participating schools.

All student disclosures or safety concerns will be handled according to school policy and applicable law. Give Miami Days staff will promptly contact the designated school personnel when required by the agreed protocol.

### Activities Measures

1. **Student workshops:** Deliver four workshops at each of up to eight schools, reaching approximately 400 students across the school year.
2. **Caregiver engagement:** Deliver at least two caregiver workshops or information sessions across participating schools, reaching approximately 80 caregivers.
3. **Facilitator and school coordination:** Brief all facilitators and designated school contacts before delivery; hold at least one implementation review per school during the year.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The project will serve students in participating Miami-Dade schools, with an estimated reach of 400 students and 80 caregivers. Schools will help select grade levels and session times based on student needs, existing support structures, and family schedules. Student participation will follow each school’s requirements for family notification, consent, and accessibility. Give Miami Days will clearly explain that workshops teach safety and help-seeking skills and do not provide counseling or collect confidential clinical histories.

School contacts will introduce the program through established family communication channels, meetings, and take-home materials. Give Miami Days will ask schools which languages families use and arrange translated materials or interpretation where resources permit. Examples and activities will be adapted to the age group and reviewed for accessibility. Caregivers will be invited to share questions before and after sessions; students will have age-appropriate ways to provide feedback.

To improve participation, the organization will coordinate with each school on timing, accessible locations, and a clear process for students who need additional support. Referral information will identify the school or community contacts families may approach. Attendance and feedback will be reviewed by school and grade band, while protecting student identity. If participation is lower at a site or a group reports that materials are not accessible or relevant, the team will consult that school and adjust scheduling, format, or examples.

## 4. Assessment

The intended improvement is that students know how to recognize common stress signals, use an age-appropriate regulation strategy, and identify trusted adults or services they can approach. Caregivers should leave with clearer information about listening to a child, contacting school supports, and locating community resources. The project will use short pre- and post-workshop knowledge checks approved by each school, session attendance records, caregiver feedback, and a brief school-contact review. It will not assess or report individual students’ mental-health diagnoses.

Across participating schools, the program aims to reach approximately 400 students and 80 caregivers. At least 80% of students completing the post-session check should correctly identify two help-seeking options; at least 75% should identify one practical strategy for managing stress or supporting a peer safely. At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents should report that they understand how to contact a school or community support. The coordinator will review results after each school cycle and meet with school contacts at least once during implementation. Findings will be summarized by school and grade band where group sizes protect privacy. The year-end report will describe reach, learning results, feedback, adaptations, and limitations; it will not claim that brief workshops alone prevent crises or establish clinical impact.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Help-seeking knowledge:** At least 80% of student respondents correctly identify two trusted help-seeking options after workshops. Measure with a brief school-approved post-session check.
2. **Coping and safe peer support:** At least 75% identify one practical self-regulation strategy or safe way to support a peer. Measure with the same post-session tool.
3. **Caregiver resource awareness:** At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents know how to contact school or community support. Measure through an anonymous post-session survey; report response rates.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Give Miami Days will assign a coordinator to manage school agreements, scheduling, facilitator preparation, family communication, safeguarding documentation, and reporting. Facilitators will receive training in age-appropriate communication, boundaries, privacy limits, and partner-specific escalation procedures. Participating schools will designate contacts who can review materials, explain school requirements, and receive referrals or concerns under the agreed protocol. Before launch, Give Miami Days will document facilitator qualifications, insurance, child-protection procedures, and each partner’s responsibilities.

**Organization-specific qualifications must be verified before submission:** Add the organization’s mission, experience delivering youth or school programming, number of students previously reached, staff credentials, safeguarding record, and confirmed school partners. If the organization has not delivered school workshops before, identify an experienced curriculum or behavioral-health partner that will review content and train facilitators.

To sustain the work, Give Miami Days will create reusable, school-reviewed materials and document the time and cost required per site. Schools and families will provide feedback on relevance and accessibility. The organization will use participation and learning results to seek future individual, corporate, and foundation support and to identify partners able to host or co-deliver sessions. Any continuation or expansion will depend on confirmed school participation, trained facilitators, funding, and alignment with school safety and student-support policies.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Students should be able to learn how to seek help regardless of family income, language, disability, or prior access to counseling. The program will be offered through partner schools at no cost to students and will use age-appropriate, plain-language materials. Give Miami Days will ask schools and caregivers about language, disability access, scheduling, and cultural considerations before delivery. Facilitators will avoid treating a student’s identity, family structure, or emotional expression as a problem and will use examples that reflect the participating community.

The project will coordinate with school staff to identify barriers to attendance and provide translated materials or interpretation where available. Students will be told what information is private and the limits that apply when a safety concern is disclosed. The team will review participation and feedback by school and grade band, with privacy safeguards, and will consult sites where attendance or reported relevance is lower. The organization will not assume which groups are most underserved; it will use partner and family input to identify priorities and adapt delivery. Any expansion of language or accessibility services will be matched to verified demand and available qualified resources.

The illustrative $65,000 request supports one school year of workshop coordination and delivery at up to eight schools. Coordinator time supports school agreements, scheduling, family communication, and implementation oversight. Facilitator and review costs support safe, consistent delivery.
