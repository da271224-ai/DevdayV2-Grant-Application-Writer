## 1. Statement of Goals

Give Miami Days will strengthen the well-being, school connection, and social-emotional skills of 60 Miami-Dade children by providing consistent, trained adult mentors and meaningful caregiver engagement. Over 12 months, at least 48 children will complete 30 or more mentoring contacts, and at least 70% of completers will improve on one or more participant-selected measures of belonging, goal-setting, or social-emotional skills. The program will connect families to qualified supports when needs exceed mentoring.

## 2. Implementation

Give Miami Days proposes a 12-month, school- and community-based mentoring program for 60 elementary- and middle-school children in Miami-Dade County. The program will pair each child with a screened, trained adult mentor or stable small-group mentor team. Mentoring will focus on relationship-building, goal-setting, communication, school connection, and age-appropriate social-emotional skills. Mentors will not provide therapy, diagnose conditions, or replace school counselors.

**Months 1–2: Partner setup and enrollment.** Confirm participating schools or community sites, referral criteria, a designated site contact, caregiver consent, information-sharing boundaries, and a written safeguarding and escalation protocol. Recruit mentors and complete required screening before any child contact. Enroll up to 60 children based on partner referrals and family interest; document baseline information and each child’s goals.

**Months 2–3: Training and matching.** Train mentors in child safeguarding, confidentiality limits, boundaries, culturally responsive communication, trauma-aware engagement, and referral procedures. Match children with mentors based on availability, interests, language needs, and caregiver input. Establish a consistent weekly meeting time and a backup contact plan for missed sessions.

**Months 3–11: Mentoring and caregiver engagement.** Provide one 60–90-minute mentoring contact per child each week during the active program calendar, through individual or stable small-group sessions at approved sites. Use structured activities and child-selected goals rather than unstructured supervision. Contact caregivers at least monthly to share attendance and progress, hear concerns, and identify participation barriers. Offer practical referrals when a family requests support or staff identify a concern within the program protocol.

**Months 6 and 12: Review and closeout.** Review participation and interim feedback with partners at month 6; adjust schedules, mentor support, and activities. At month 12, conduct end-of-program measures, gather caregiver and mentor feedback, review referrals and completion, and prepare a results and learning report. Staff will promptly follow partner safety procedures for any urgent concern.

### Activities Measures

1. **Mentoring contacts:** Deliver weekly 60–90-minute contacts during the active program calendar to up to 60 children; target at least 48 children completing 30 or more contacts.
2. **Mentor preparation and support:** Screen and train all assigned mentors before matching; provide monthly mentor check-ins and documented supervision throughout the program.
3. **Caregiver engagement:** Contact each participating family at least monthly and offer at least four caregiver connection sessions or workshops during the year; record attendance, requests, and referrals.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The program will serve up to 60 elementary- and middle-school children who attend participating Miami-Dade schools or community sites and could benefit from a consistent, supportive adult relationship. Priority will be given to children referred by partner staff or caregivers because of a need for stronger school connection, trusted-adult support, goal-setting practice, or social-emotional skill development. Participation will be voluntary and require caregiver consent and child assent. The program will not use a diagnosis as an eligibility requirement.

Give Miami Days will work with school and community partners to identify appropriate referral routes and explain the program directly to caregivers. Outreach will use partner meetings, family communication channels, community events, and short program materials in the languages families use, subject to verified translation capacity. Staff will describe what mentoring includes and does not include, the expected schedule, privacy limits, safety procedures, and how families can raise concerns before enrollment.

To reduce barriers, staff will ask families about preferred meeting times, language, transportation, accessibility, and other participation needs. Where the approved budget and partner arrangements allow, the program will offer transportation or participation support. Caregivers will help shape each child’s goals and may request a different match or withdrawal without penalty. The program will seek feedback from children and caregivers at intake, midyear, and closeout, and use it to adjust meeting format and outreach. All recruitment and service decisions will follow applicable nondiscrimination, safeguarding, and school-partner requirements.

## 4. Assessment

The program’s intended improvement is that participating children experience stronger connection to a trusted adult and school/community setting, practice age-appropriate social-emotional skills, and become more confident setting goals and seeking help. The team will establish a baseline for each participant during enrollment and repeat brief, age-appropriate measures at midyear and closeout. Measures will focus on belonging, goal-setting, and social-emotional skill confidence; the specific instruments will be selected with partner input before enrollment and used consistently. Mentoring attendance, caregiver contacts, mentor retention, referrals offered, and program completion will be tracked throughout the year.

The program aims to enroll 60 children, have at least 48 complete 30 or more mentoring contacts, and have at least 70% of completers improve on one or more selected measures from baseline to closeout. At least 80% of caregivers who respond to the closeout survey should report that the program was accessible and that they understood how to request additional support. The project coordinator will review progress monthly, discuss barriers with partners quarterly, and adjust scheduling or mentor support as needed. Results will be reported in aggregate; individual information will be shared only as permitted by consent, law, and partner safeguarding procedures. The team will interpret changes cautiously because this is a small, non-randomized community program and cannot establish that mentoring alone caused an observed change.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Sustained participation:** At least 48 of 60 enrolled children complete 30 or more mentoring contacts. Measure through dated attendance and contact logs, reviewed monthly.
2. **Child-reported progress:** At least 70% of program completers improve on one or more baseline-to-closeout measures of belonging, goal-setting, or social-emotional skill confidence. Measure with the same age-appropriate questionnaire at intake, midyear, and closeout.
3. **Caregiver experience:** At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents report that the program was accessible and that they know how to request additional support. Measure through a brief closeout survey and summarize response rate with results.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Give Miami Days will implement the program through a designated program coordinator, screened mentors, and written agreements with participating schools or community sites. The coordinator will manage enrollment, mentor preparation, caregiver communication, safeguarding documentation, attendance, referrals, and reporting. Partner site staff will advise on scheduling and referral fit; qualified mental-health providers will receive referrals when a child or family needs clinical assessment or treatment. Before launch, the organization will document its child-protection policy, screening process, supervision plan, insurance coverage, and partner roles.

**Organization-specific qualifications must be completed before submission:** Add Give Miami Days’ verified mission statement; years of service; relevant mentoring or youth-program experience; number and demographics of people served; staff and board oversight; existing safeguarding procedures; and confirmed school, community, or clinical partners. Do not state that these systems, results, or partnerships exist until the organization has verified them. If the organization is new to mentoring, identify the experienced partner that will train or supervise delivery.

Sustainability will be pursued through a mix of individual giving, Give Miami Day campaign proceeds, foundation and corporate requests, volunteer recruitment, and continued school/community partnerships. During the grant period, the coordinator will document program costs per participant, mentor retention, attendance, caregiver feedback, and outcomes to inform future fundraising and capacity decisions. The organization will assess continuation with partners before the final quarter and will not promise expansion until funding, staffing, and safeguarding capacity are confirmed.

## 6. Advancing Equity

The program is designed to reduce barriers to supportive adult relationships for children whose families may have limited access to extracurricular mentoring or other community supports. Enrollment will be based on child and family interest and partner referrals, not ability to pay or a clinical diagnosis. Give Miami Days will invite caregivers and children to identify goals, preferred language, scheduling needs, and what a respectful mentor relationship looks like for them. Matching will consider language, interests, identity-related preferences shared by the family, and accessibility needs to the extent the available mentor pool allows.

To make participation more equitable, the program will use familiar school or community locations, explain expectations in accessible language, seek to provide translated materials, and address transportation or scheduling barriers where funds and partners permit. Children and caregivers can raise concerns or request a rematch. Staff will review participation and completion data by age, site, language preference, and other voluntarily provided demographic information, with privacy safeguards and small-group suppression. The team will use differences in enrollment, retention, or reported experience to adjust outreach and delivery. The organization will confirm its specific priority communities with local partners rather than assume that one approach fits every Miami-Dade neighborhood.

## 7. Financial Narrative

The illustrative request of $75,000 supports a 12-month mentoring cohort for up to 60 children. The proposed allocation is: $28,000 for a part-time program coordinator, including applicable benefits; $12,000 for mentor screening, training, and supervision; $8,000 for activity materials and child-development resources; $8,000 for family transportation and participation support; $7,000 for evaluation tools, data management, and reporting; and $12,000 for site, insurance, and allocated administrative costs. These amounts total $75,000 and are planning estimates to be reconciled with Give Miami Days’ actual compensation, vendor quotes, indirect-cost policy, and operating budget.

The coordinator allocation supports enrollment, partner coordination, safeguarding oversight, caregiver engagement, and tracking. Screening, training, and supervision help ensure safe and consistent mentor relationships. Materials support structured sessions; family supports address access barriers; evaluation funding provides basic outcome tracking and accountability. Site and administrative costs enable delivery and financial controls. Before submission, Give Miami Days should attach the budget format required for the active cycle and show how any other revenue or in-kind support contributes to the work. The Miami Foundation currently describes Community Grants as two-year, mission-building general operating support; if that structure remains in the next cycle, this narrative should be revised to explain how unrestricted support strengthens the organization’s overall capacity and mission, rather than imply a restricted project grant. The request amount and all cost allocations remain subject to the next cycle’s guidelines and the organization’s approved budget.
