## 1. Statement of Goals

Give Miami Days will equip students, caregivers, and school partners at up to 10 Miami-Dade schools with practical tools for personal safety, emotional resilience, and help-seeking. During one school year, the program will reach approximately 500 students and 100 caregivers; at least 80% of student respondents will identify two trusted help-seeking options after participating in workshops.

## 2. Implementation

Safe, Seen, Supported is a school-based prevention and awareness initiative. It will provide age-appropriate workshops for students and caregivers, share clear help-seeking information, and connect schools to partner resources. Activities will complement each school’s existing safety, counseling, and crisis-response systems. Give Miami Days will not conduct investigations, diagnose students, or substitute workshops for clinical or emergency services.

**Months 1–2: Partner readiness.** Confirm up to 10 school partners, grade bands, designated student-support contacts, family notification and consent requirements, language and accessibility needs, and protocols for disclosures or urgent concerns. Have qualified professionals and school representatives review workshop content and referrals.

**Months 2–3: Preparation and scheduling.** Train facilitators in age-appropriate delivery, boundaries, confidentiality limits, safeguarding, and site-specific escalation steps. Schedule sessions with schools and establish baseline knowledge questions approved by partner contacts.

**Months 3–9: Student and caregiver workshops.** Deliver three student workshops at each participating school, covering recognizing unsafe situations, emotional safety, stress signals, self-regulation, identifying trusted adults, peer-support boundaries, and help-seeking. Offer caregiver sessions on listening, responding to concerns, and contacting school or community resources. Provide take-home information that lists verified local and school support contacts.

**Months 9–10: Evaluation and improvement.** Collect attendance, post-session knowledge checks, caregiver feedback, and school-contact feedback. Review results with partners and identify needed changes to materials, language access, scheduling, or referral clarity. Prepare an aggregate closeout report.

All safety disclosures will be handled under partner policy and applicable law. The program will communicate its privacy limits before workshops begin.

### Activities Measures

1. **Student sessions:** Deliver three sessions at up to 10 schools, reaching approximately 500 students; track attendance by site and grade band.
2. **Caregiver engagement:** Offer at least one caregiver session or information event per participating school, targeting 100 caregivers overall.
3. **School readiness:** Confirm a designated contact and reviewed referral/escalation information at every participating site before delivery.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The initiative will serve students at up to 10 Miami-Dade schools and their caregivers. Each school will help determine the grade groups, session schedule, and appropriate family outreach. Student participation will follow the school’s notification, consent, and accessibility requirements. Caregivers will be invited through established school communication channels and offered clear information about the workshop purpose, facilitators, and referral options.

Give Miami Days will consult school staff and families about language, age relevance, disability access, and sensitive topics before delivering sessions. Materials will use plain language and identify the limits of confidentiality and the school contacts available for follow-up. Translation or interpretation will be arranged where qualified resources are available. Sessions will not ask students to disclose personal trauma or describe unsafe experiences publicly.

Students and caregivers will be invited to provide feedback in anonymous or age-appropriate formats. The coordinator will work with sites to adjust timing or format when participation is low. Attendance and feedback will be reviewed across sites without identifying individual students. Schools will receive current resource information to share after the event, and families will be told how to contact support privately.

## 4. Assessment

The project seeks to improve students’ ability to identify trusted help-seeking options and practical safety or self-regulation strategies, while helping caregivers understand how to connect a child with school and community support. Partner-approved pre- or post-session knowledge checks will measure learning. Attendance records will document reach; caregiver surveys and school-contact feedback will assess relevance, clarity, and accessibility. The project will not collect clinical information or treat workshop responses as diagnostic data.

The target is to reach approximately 500 students and 100 caregivers. At least 80% of student respondents should identify two trusted help-seeking options, and at least 75% should identify one practical safety or stress-management strategy. At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents should know how to contact a school or community support. The coordinator will review data by school and grade band when group size protects privacy. Quarterly partner discussions will identify material or referral changes. The final report will include reach, response rates, feedback, and limitations. The organization will not claim that one-time workshops prevent violence or replace school safety and mental-health services.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Help-seeking knowledge:** At least 80% of student respondents identify two trusted options. Measure with a school-approved post-session check.
2. **Practical skills:** At least 75% identify one safety or stress-management strategy. Measure through the same check.
3. **Caregiver resource awareness:** At least 80% of caregiver respondents know how to contact support. Measure through an anonymous survey and report response rate.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Give Miami Days will designate a coordinator to manage school agreements, schedules, facilitator preparation, family communications, safeguarding documentation, and reporting. School partners will designate contacts to review materials and explain referral and escalation procedures. Qualified professionals will review content and advise on support resources. Facilitators will be trained on age-appropriate practice and the limits of the workshop role. The organization will document insurance, consent requirements, privacy, and incident-response procedures before delivery.

If the organization has not led school safety workshops, identify the experienced partner providing curriculum review or facilitator training. Do not claim access to student-support systems without school confirmation.

To sustain the initiative, the organization will maintain school-reviewed materials and a verified resource list, track cost and reach per site, and gather school and family feedback. Findings will guide future revisions and fundraising to individual donors, foundations, and local businesses. Schools may continue sharing materials after the grant period, but ongoing workshops will require renewed partner participation, trained facilitators, and secured funding. The organization will use the pilot to determine a feasible delivery scale rather than assume expansion.

## 6. Advancing Equity

School-based workshops can reach children who may not independently seek counseling or have access to private services. The initiative will be offered through participating schools without a fee or diagnosis requirement. Give Miami Days will consult school staff and caregivers on language, accessibility, grade suitability, and safe ways to discuss sensitive topics. Students will not be asked to share personal experiences in front of peers, and materials will offer more than one route to seek help.

The organization will provide plain-language resources and seek translation or interpretation where qualified support is available. Sessions will be scheduled with schools to reduce conflicts and held in accessible spaces. The coordinator will monitor attendance and feedback by school and grade band, protecting privacy, and ask sites with lower participation what would make delivery more useful. Families and students will be able to provide anonymous feedback. Priority communities and adaptations will be identified with local partners rather than inferred from broad demographic assumptions.
