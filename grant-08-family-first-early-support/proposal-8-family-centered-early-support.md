## 1. Statement of Goals

Give Miami Days will support 55 Miami-Dade families by pairing child mentoring with caregiver counseling or support and school workshops on safety and mental health. By month 12, at least 44 families will complete an agreed support plan, and at least 75% of caregiver respondents will report greater confidence finding support and communicating with their child. Children will practice help-seeking and emotional coping skills.

## 2. Implementation

Family First organizes services around each household’s priorities. The program will serve 55 families with school-age children and combine child mentoring, caregiver counseling or support through qualified providers, and school-based safety and mental-health workshops. Families will choose which services they use, and the coordinator will help connect those services without requiring a family to repeat its story to multiple contacts. The program is supportive and educational; clinical care is provided only by qualified professionals.

**Months 1–2: Build the service pathway.** Confirm school/community referral partners, qualified counseling providers, consent and information-sharing rules, privacy safeguards, and urgent-safety procedures. Define what the coordinator can document and what remains within the provider’s clinical record. Recruit and screen mentors and confirm workshop content.

**Months 2–3: Family intake.** Invite families through trusted partners. Conduct a family-led intake to identify strengths, priorities, language and access needs, preferred schedule, and desired support. Agree on a short support plan with one or two goals and explain how to change or pause services.

**Months 3–10: Deliver and coordinate.** Match children to mentors for weekly contacts, targeting 20 or more contacts for families completing the program. Offer caregiver counseling or support sessions through qualified providers based on need, family choice, and service availability. Deliver monthly school or community workshops on child safety, stress, emotional regulation, trusted adults, and help-seeking. The coordinator will check in monthly and help families navigate referrals with consent.

**Months 6 and 11–12: Family review and closeout.** Invite families to review goals at month 6 and adjust the plan. At closeout, gather caregiver and child feedback, review completion and referral connections, and prepare an aggregate report. Safeguarding concerns will follow partner procedures and applicable law.

### Activities Measures

1. **Family-led support plans:** Complete an intake and agreed plan with up to 55 families; review each plan at least once midyear.
2. **Mentoring and counseling/support:** Offer weekly mentor contact and qualified caregiver/family support; target 44 families completing their selected plan.
3. **Workshops:** Hold at least 10 school or community workshops during the year and record attendance and learning checks.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The program will serve 55 families with school-age children in Miami-Dade County. Families may be referred by schools, family-serving organizations, or community providers, or contact the program through partner outreach. Enrollment will be voluntary and will not require a diagnosis. The intake process will begin with family strengths and priorities rather than a predetermined service package.

The coordinator will explain mentoring, provider-delivered counseling or support, workshops, privacy limits, and the family’s ability to select or decline each element. Caregivers and children will identify goals and preferred ways to participate. Outreach will use trusted partners, plain-language materials, and family communication channels. Give Miami Days will ask about preferred language, scheduling, transportation, childcare, disability access, and interpretation needs and arrange support where available.

Workshops will be held at familiar school or community sites. Caregivers will be invited to provide feedback and suggest topics. The coordinator will contact families monthly to check whether the agreed plan remains useful, whether access barriers have changed, and whether another referral is desired. Families may pause, change, or end participation without penalty. Aggregate enrollment and completion patterns will be reviewed with partners, and outreach will be adapted with family input when groups are not being reached.

## 4. Assessment

The intended improvement is that families experience a clearer, more coordinated route to support, while children gain a stable mentoring relationship and practical safety and help-seeking skills. At intake, families will identify one or two goals and rate their confidence navigating support and communicating about the child’s needs. The same brief questions will be offered at closeout. Staff will track support-plan completion, mentor contacts, provider sessions offered and attended, workshop participation, referral requests, and connection steps when families consent to follow-up.

The program aims to enroll 55 families, have at least 44 complete their agreed support plan, and have at least 75% of caregiver respondents report increased confidence in finding support or communicating with their child. At least 70% of youth workshop respondents should correctly identify a trusted adult or help-seeking option. The coordinator will review participation monthly and discuss service gaps with partners quarterly. Results will be reported with survey response rates and in aggregate. Counseling records will remain with qualified providers. The organization will describe observed change and family experience, not infer clinical improvement from service participation alone.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Family plan completion:** At least 44 of 55 families complete their agreed support plan. Measure with service records and family confirmation.
2. **Caregiver confidence:** At least 75% of caregiver respondents report improved confidence finding support or communicating with their child. Measure with intake and closeout questions.
3. **Youth help-seeking knowledge:** At least 70% of workshop respondents identify a trusted adult or help-seeking option. Measure with an age-appropriate post-workshop check.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Give Miami Days will assign a coordinator to maintain the family-led intake process, partner relationships, mentor scheduling, workshop logistics, referral follow-up, and reporting. Qualified providers will deliver counseling and advise on clinical referrals. Schools and community organizations will assist with outreach and identify appropriate site contacts. Mentors will be screened, trained, and supervised. Prior to launch, the organization will document consent, privacy, safeguarding, insurance, and clear boundaries between coordination and clinical care.

**Verify before submission:** Add Give Miami Days’ mission, relevant family-serving experience, staff and mentor qualifications, current service reach, past results, and confirmed provider and school partners. Do not assert clinical capacity or a history of successful results without records. If no qualified provider is contracted, identify the provider and confirm service availability before submitting.

For sustainability, the program will document the family-led planning process, costs per family, participation and completion, caregiver feedback, and referral demand. Reusable intake and workshop materials may reduce future setup costs. Give Miami Days will seek individual donations, foundation and corporate support, and continuing partner contributions. The organization will use family and provider feedback to determine whether the model should continue or be adapted. Continuation depends on funding, qualified provider capacity, and adequate supervision.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Families often navigate different systems with different eligibility rules, language, schedules, and points of contact. This program will provide one coordination point and let families define their priorities. There will be no fee or diagnosis requirement. At intake, staff will ask families what has made support difficult to access and what would make participation workable. Children and caregivers will be treated as partners in setting goals, selecting program components, and deciding whether referrals are appropriate.

Give Miami Days will use trusted community locations and plain-language communication, and will arrange translation, interpretation, accessibility, transportation, or childcare support where qualified resources and budget allow. The program will explain privacy limits and avoid sharing family information beyond consent and legal requirements. Staff will review enrollment, service completion, and satisfaction by site and voluntarily provided access information while protecting small groups. If families report that a process is confusing or inaccessible, the coordinator will consult them and partners about a practical change. Priority communities will be defined through local family input rather than assumption.

## 7. Financial Narrative

The illustrative $95,000 request supports 12 months of coordinated service for 55 families. The planning allocation is $30,000 for a part-time family coordinator, including applicable benefits; $28,000 for qualified counseling or family-support services; $14,000 for mentor screening, training, and supervision; $8,000 for school and caregiver workshop materials; $7,000 for transportation, interpretation, and childcare support; $4,000 for evaluation and data management; and $4,000 for insurance and administrative allocation. These estimates total $95,000 and must be reconciled with actual rates, provider capacity, and the organization’s operating budget.

The coordinator supports a family-led plan and reduces duplicated navigation. Provider costs fund qualified services; mentor costs support safe child relationships. Workshop and access funds make participation practical, while evaluation funding supports outcome tracking.
