## 1. Statement of Goals

Tidepath Family Alliance will provide 70 children with reliable adult mentors, caregiver support and school workshops on safety and mental-health awareness. By the end of 12 months, at least 56 children will complete 25 mentoring contacts, and at least 70% of completers will report improvement in one selected area of belonging, communication, or goal-setting. Caregivers will receive practical information and referral options.

## 2. Implementation

Trusted Adults, Stronger Futures is a year-long mentoring program that surrounds each child with a consistent trained adult while keeping caregivers and school partners involved. The program will serve 70 children in Miami-Dade County. Mentors will support relationship-building, communication, school connection, and child-selected goals. Caregivers will be offered family support or counseling through qualified providers, and schools will host safety and mental-health awareness workshops. Mentoring is not clinical treatment.

**Months 1–2: Recruit and establish partner protocols.** Confirm school or community sites, enrollment criteria, family consent and child assent, confidentiality limits, child-protection processes, and designated contacts for concerns. Recruit mentors and complete required screening. Confirm qualified provider capacity for family support and referral needs.

**Months 2–3: Prepare and match.** Train mentors in boundaries, child safeguarding, listening, inclusive communication, trauma-aware engagement, and referral procedures. Invite caregivers and children to identify interests, goals, language needs, and match preferences. Match each child to a mentor or stable small group and set a consistent weekly schedule.

**Months 3–10: Mentoring and complementary activities.** Offer weekly mentor contacts with structured, age-appropriate activities. Target 25 completed contacts for each completing child. Provide quarterly school workshops on personal safety, emotional safety, recognizing stress, coping strategies, trusted adults, and safe help-seeking. Offer caregiver sessions and connect families with qualified counseling or support services when requested. The coordinator will conduct monthly mentor check-ins and caregiver communication.

**Months 6 and 11–12: Review and closeout.** Review attendance, match stability, family input, and referrals at midyear. Rematch or adjust schedules when requested. At closeout, collect child and caregiver feedback, repeat selected measures, and prepare an aggregate report and continuation plan. Any urgent concern will follow partner procedures and applicable law.

### Activities Measures

1. **Mentoring:** Match 70 children and provide weekly contact; target 56 completing at least 25 contacts.
2. **Safety and mental-health workshops:** Conduct quarterly workshops with participating school or community partners and track attendance and learning checks.
3. **Caregiver support:** Offer at least four caregiver sessions and provide qualified-provider referral navigation to families requesting additional help.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The program will enroll up to 70 children from participating Miami-Dade schools and community sites, with caregiver involvement throughout the year. School and community partners will share information with families and refer children who may benefit from a stable adult relationship and structured opportunities to practice communication and goal-setting. Enrollment will be voluntary, based on interest and program fit, and will not require a mental-health diagnosis.

Before matching, staff will meet with caregivers and children to explain the mentor’s role, program schedule, privacy limits, safeguarding procedures, and options for workshops or family support. Children and caregivers will share interests, preferred language, access needs, and match preferences. Tidepath Family Alliance will use partner communication channels, family meetings, and plain-language materials. Translated information or interpretation will be provided where qualified resources are available.

Mentoring will take place at approved, familiar locations and follow the agreed supervision rules. The coordinator will contact caregivers monthly and provide a clear route to raise concerns or request a rematch. Workshop times will be coordinated with schools and families. Staff will monitor enrollment and completion by site and age group, protect personal information, and seek partner and family input when participation barriers emerge.

## 4. Assessment

The program aims to establish sustained, supportive adult relationships and help children practice communication, goal-setting, and safe help-seeking. At enrollment, children will select a personal goal with caregiver input and complete a short age-appropriate baseline measure in belonging, communication, or goal-setting. At closeout, the same measure will be offered. Staff will track match start and stability, mentoring contacts, workshop participation, caregiver engagement, and referrals requested.

The program aims to enroll 70 children, have at least 56 complete 25 contacts, and have at least 70% of completers improve on one selected measure. At least 80% of caregivers who complete a survey should report that they understood the mentor role and knew how to raise a concern or request support. Monthly coordinator reviews will identify missed contacts, match challenges, and safety issues. Quarterly partner reviews will guide schedule and workshop adjustments. Results will be reported in aggregate with response rates and limitations. Participant progress will be interpreted as an observed change during the program, not proof that mentoring caused the change.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Mentoring completion:** At least 56 of 70 children complete 25 contacts. Measure through dated logs and match records.
2. **Child-selected progress:** At least 70% of completers improve on one selected measure. Measure with baseline and closeout questions using the same tool.
3. **Caregiver understanding:** At least 80% of caregiver survey respondents understand the mentor role and how to raise a concern. Measure through a closeout survey and report response rate.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Tidepath Family Alliance will appoint a coordinator responsible for mentor recruitment, screening, training records, matching, supervision, family communication, partner coordination, and evaluation. Schools or community sites will provide designated contacts and approved spaces. Qualified providers will deliver any counseling or advise on referrals. Mentors will receive defined role descriptions and regular support. Before launch, the organization will verify its safeguarding, insurance, confidentiality, and incident-response procedures and document partner responsibilities.

To sustain the program, Tidepath Family Alliance will track mentor retention, child completion, caregiver experience, and cost per match. It will build a repeatable training package, maintain partner relationships, and seek individual, corporate, and foundation support. Schools and families will be asked whether the model remains useful and feasible. The organization will continue or expand only when funding, supervision, and screened mentor capacity are secured.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Children do not have equal access to a reliable adult mentor, family support, or clear information about safety and mental health. The program will be free, voluntary, and open without a diagnosis requirement. Families will help choose the child’s goals, mentor preferences, schedule, and participation format. Matching will consider language, interests, accessibility, and preferences shared by the family, within the available screened mentor pool.

Tidepath Family Alliance will recruit mentors through community partners and explain the program in plain language. Familiar school or community locations can reduce transportation and trust barriers. The coordinator will ask about accommodations and language needs and arrange support where qualified resources and budget allow. Families may raise concerns or request a rematch. The organization will review who enrolls, completes mentoring, and reports a positive experience, protecting identity and avoiding conclusions from small samples. Where gaps appear, staff will consult families and partners and adjust recruitment, matching, or schedules. Priority groups will be identified with local input.
