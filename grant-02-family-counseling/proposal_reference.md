## 2. Implementation

Tidepath Family Alliance proposes a 12-month family support and service-navigation program for approximately 50 Miami-Dade households with children. The program will combine needs screening, brief family support or counseling delivered by qualified providers, caregiver learning sessions, and follow-up referrals. Tidepath Family Alliance staff will coordinate participation and access; clinical assessment and treatment will remain the responsibility of appropriately licensed or otherwise qualified providers.

**Months 1–2: Partner and protocol setup.** Confirm provider and community referral partners, eligibility and referral pathways, language capacity, consent and privacy procedures, and a written protocol for urgent safety concerns. Establish service availability and payment terms with qualified providers. Prepare accessible enrollment and feedback materials.

**Months 2–3: Outreach and enrollment.** Invite referrals from participating schools, pediatric or community partners, and families. Complete an initial needs and access assessment with each household, including preferred language, scheduling, transportation, and requested support. Develop a family plan and explain the scope and limits of program services.

**Months 3–10: Family services.** Offer brief support sessions or counseling through qualified providers, based on assessed need and available capacity. Provide monthly caregiver workshops on listening and communication, responding to stress, establishing supportive routines, and navigating community resources. Staff will check in with families after referrals and assist with appointment navigation when requested. Families requiring longer-term or specialized care will be referred to suitable providers; the program will not promise services outside its capacity.

**Months 11–12: Follow-up and learning.** Complete follow-up with enrolled families, gather caregiver feedback, review referral connection and participation data, and prepare a results report. The program coordinator and providers will review access barriers quarterly and adjust outreach or scheduling where feasible.

### Activities Measures

1. **Family intake and planning:** Assess up to 50 households and document a support plan and access preferences for each enrolled family.
2. **Support and counseling:** Offer qualified-provider sessions; target at least 40 families completing two or more sessions, subject to provider capacity and family choice.
3. **Caregiver workshops and navigation:** Hold at least 10 workshops during the year and provide referral navigation and follow-up to families requesting additional services.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The program will serve approximately 50 Miami-Dade households with children experiencing emotional, behavioral, or family stress and whose caregivers want support or help navigating services. Families may learn about the program through participating schools, community organizations, pediatric or behavioral-health partners, and direct community outreach. Participation will be voluntary; an initial conversation will clarify family goals, program scope, privacy limits, and available service capacity.

Outreach will use trusted community partners and plain-language materials. Tidepath Family Alliance will confirm the languages families prefer and provide translated materials or interpretation through qualified resources where available. Staff will ask caregivers about scheduling, transportation, accessibility, and childcare barriers and will use the approved budget and partner capacity to reduce those barriers. Workshops will be offered at accessible community or school locations and, when feasible, remotely.

Caregivers will help define goals and choose whether to participate in workshops, support sessions, or referrals. A family may decline a referral or stop participating without penalty. Staff will explain that urgent safety concerns follow established safeguarding procedures and that the program is not an emergency or substitute for ongoing clinical care. Feedback at intake, after services, and at closeout will guide improvements to outreach and delivery.

## 4. Assessment

The program aims to improve caregivers’ confidence in responding to children’s needs and help families connect with appropriate support sooner. At intake, staff will document the family’s requested goals, access preferences, and current service-navigation needs. At closeout, caregivers will be invited to complete the same brief confidence questions and a satisfaction survey. The coordinator will track enrollment, sessions offered and attended, workshop participation, referral requests, and whether families report making a connection with a referred service. The program will not collect clinical details beyond what is necessary for safe coordination and reporting.

Targets are to enroll approximately 50 households; have at least 40 complete two or more support sessions; have at least 75% of caregiver survey respondents report improved confidence in supporting their child; and have at least 70% of families who request a referral report completing a connection step, such as contacting or scheduling with a provider. The coordinator will review de-identified progress data quarterly with service partners, identify where families encounter delays, and adjust navigation or workshop schedules. Results will be reported in aggregate with survey response rates. Because participation is voluntary and the project is not a clinical trial, results will describe observed changes and service access rather than claim that the program caused clinical improvement.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Family participation:** At least 40 of approximately 50 enrolled households complete two or more support sessions. Measure through confidential service logs, reviewed monthly.
2. **Caregiver confidence:** At least 75% of caregiver survey respondents report increased confidence in supporting their child. Measure with a brief intake and closeout questionnaire.
3. **Referral connection:** At least 70% of families requesting referrals report completing one connection step. Measure through voluntary follow-up; report the number of families eligible to respond.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Families can face financial, language, transportation, scheduling, disability-access, and information barriers when seeking support for a child. The program will not require a diagnosis or ability to pay for participation. Outreach through trusted schools and community partners will help families learn about the program in familiar settings. Intake will ask each caregiver about preferred language, accessibility, scheduling, and the kind of support they consider useful; families will help set their own goals and choose among available services.

Tidepath Family Alliance will use plain-language explanations, seek translation or interpretation where available, and offer flexible scheduling or participation support within the approved budget. Referrals will be based on family preference and provider fit, not assumptions about a household.
