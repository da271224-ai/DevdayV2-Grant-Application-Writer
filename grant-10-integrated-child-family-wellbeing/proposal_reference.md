## 2. Implementation

Stronger Together is a 12-month coordinated support model for 100 children and caregivers at five Miami-Dade school or community sites. Families will be offered mentoring for children, counseling or support sessions for caregivers through qualified providers, and workshops at schools on safety, stress, mental-health awareness, and help-seeking. A designated coordinator will connect the components around family-selected goals. The program will not substitute mentoring or workshops for clinical treatment or emergency response.

**Months 1–2: Site and provider agreements.** Confirm five sites, designated contacts, referral criteria, service capacity, consent, confidentiality, accessibility, and urgent-safety protocols. Secure qualified counseling partners and define the coordinator’s role in referral follow-up. Screen and prepare mentors and facilitators.

**Months 2–3: Enrollment and baseline.** Enroll up to 100 children and caregivers through partner referrals and outreach. Obtain caregiver consent and child assent. Ask each family to identify a goal and preferred services. Record baseline measures of belonging, coping, or help-seeking using an age-appropriate tool selected with partner input.

**Months 3–10: Coordinated service delivery.** Provide weekly mentor contacts to a cohort of approximately 70 children, targeting at least 24 contacts for completers. Offer qualified family support or counseling based on caregiver choice and provider availability. Deliver at least four school workshops per site on personal and emotional safety, recognizing stress, coping, trusted adults, and referral options. The coordinator will check in with each family monthly, identify access barriers, and support referrals with consent.

**Months 6 and 11–12: Learning and closeout.** Convene site partners at midyear to review attendance and service gaps. At closeout, repeat participant measures, collect family feedback, review budget and referral connections, and prepare an aggregate report. All safety concerns will follow site procedures and applicable law.

### Activities Measures

1. **Mentoring:** Offer weekly contacts to approximately 70 children; target 56 completing 24 or more contacts.
2. **Family support:** Offer qualified-provider support to families requesting it; track sessions and referrals with consent.
3. **School workshops:** Deliver at least four workshops at each of five sites and track attendance and post-session knowledge checks.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The project will serve up to 100 children and caregivers at five participating Miami-Dade schools or community sites. Families may be referred by partner staff or learn about the program through community outreach. Participation will be voluntary, free to families, and not dependent on a diagnosis. The mentoring component will serve a defined cohort; workshops and family activities may reach additional enrolled households depending on site capacity.

Tidepath Family Alliance will explain each service, who delivers it, privacy limits, and referral procedures before enrollment. Caregivers and children will identify their own goals and select which components they want. Outreach will use trusted school and community channels, family meetings, and plain-language materials. Staff will ask about preferred language, schedule, transportation, disability access, and childcare needs. Translation, interpretation, or participation support will be arranged where qualified resources and budget allow.

The coordinator will serve as a consistent family contact, check in monthly, and seek feedback after workshops and provider referrals. Families may decline one service while continuing with others or may withdraw without penalty. Partner contacts will review participation and accessibility by site. If families face barriers or find services disconnected, the coordinator will consult them and partners about adjustments. Personally identifying or clinical information will not be used in public reporting.

## 4. Assessment

The model seeks to make support easier to access and more connected to each family’s priorities. Children are expected to strengthen their relationship with a trusted adult and improve safety or help-seeking knowledge; caregivers are expected to understand more clearly how to request support. Staff will collect an intake measure and closeout measure on one child-selected area, along with attendance, mentoring contacts, caregiver sessions, workshops, and referral connection steps where consent permits. Caregiver and site feedback will identify whether coordination was helpful.

Targets are to enroll 100 children, have at least 80 complete their selected support pathway, and have at least 70% of participants with matched measures improve on one area of belonging, coping, or help-seeking. At least 75% of caregiver respondents should report that they know how to request additional support. The coordinator will review monthly activity data and convene partners quarterly to identify gaps. The year-end report will present aggregate reach, completion, measures, survey response rates, and lessons. Clinical records remain with providers. The organization will report observed outcomes without attributing changes solely to this program or claiming clinical treatment effects.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Pathway completion:** At least 80 of 100 enrolled children complete the services selected in their support plan. Measure through participation records and family confirmation.
2. **Child progress:** At least 70% of children with matched measures improve on one selected area. Measure with the same age-appropriate baseline and closeout tool.
3. **Caregiver navigation:** At least 75% of caregiver respondents know how to request support. Measure through a closeout survey and report response rate.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Children and families can encounter unequal access to supportive adults, counseling, and understandable school resources. The program will be free and will not require a diagnosis. Families will set goals and choose which services they want, helping ensure that support reflects their priorities rather than a one-size-fits-all model. Trusted schools and community sites will provide familiar places to learn about and access the program.

Tidepath Family Alliance will ask families about language, disability access, transportation, schedule, and childcare needs and provide reasonable support where resources allow. Materials will use plain language and explain privacy and safety boundaries. Families can decline a service, request a mentor change, or raise concerns without losing access to other components. Staff will review enrollment, completion, referral access, and satisfaction by site and voluntarily provided demographic information when privacy permits. Where disparities or barriers emerge, staff will consult families and partners and adapt outreach or delivery. The organization will identify priority populations with local input rather than relying on assumptions.
