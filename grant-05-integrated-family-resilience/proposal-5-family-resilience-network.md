## 1. Statement of Goals

Give Miami Days will coordinate mentoring, caregiver support, school-based learning, and referrals for approximately 120 Miami-Dade children and their families. Over 12 months, at least 90 children will complete their planned support pathway, and at least 70% of participating children will improve on one selected measure of belonging, help-seeking, or social-emotional skills. The project will strengthen families’ ability to find appropriate support through four partner sites.

## 2. Implementation

Give Miami Days proposes a 12-month integrated support pilot at four Miami-Dade school or community sites, serving approximately 120 children and their caregivers. The model combines consistent mentoring, caregiver engagement and navigation, school-based workshops, and referral to qualified providers when a child or family needs support beyond the program’s scope. A coordinator will manage enrollment and continuity across components. Mentors and workshop facilitators will not provide clinical treatment unless separately qualified and contracted.

**Months 1–2: Partnership and readiness.** Confirm four sites, referral and enrollment criteria, staff contacts, family consent, information-sharing limits, accessibility, and safeguarding and escalation protocols. Secure qualified providers for curriculum review and referral coordination. Recruit and screen mentors; confirm schedules, sites, and transportation supports.

**Months 2–3: Enrollment and planning.** Enroll approximately 120 children through partner referrals and caregiver outreach. Gather caregiver consent and child assent, document access needs, and identify a child-selected goal. Assign each participant a primary support contact and explain available components and referral limits.

**Months 3–10: Integrated delivery.** Offer weekly mentoring contacts to a cohort of approximately 60 children, with structured activities and consistent adult matches. Deliver age-appropriate school or community workshops on emotional safety, stress management, and help-seeking to participating groups. Provide caregiver information sessions and support navigation for families requesting help. Coordinate referrals to qualified providers with family consent and according to safety protocols. Review attendance and barriers monthly with site contacts.

**Months 6 and 11–12: Review and closeout.** At month 6, review participation, caregiver feedback, referral challenges, and site operations; adapt schedules and support. During the final quarter, complete endline measures, gather feedback, reconcile costs, and prepare an aggregate report with continuation recommendations.

### Activities Measures

1. **Mentoring:** Provide weekly contacts to an approximately 60-child cohort; target at least 45 children completing 30 or more contacts.
2. **School/community workshops:** Deliver at least four workshop sessions at each of four partner sites, with estimated reach included in site-level attendance records.
3. **Family support and navigation:** Offer at least eight caregiver sessions during the year and document family requests and referral connection steps, with consent.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The pilot will serve approximately 120 children and caregivers connected to four participating Miami-Dade schools or community sites. The mentoring component will initially serve a cohort of approximately 60 children, while workshops and caregiver activities may reach a broader group, subject to site capacity. Priority will be given to families who express interest and are referred by trusted partner staff because they want stronger adult support, school connection, caregiver guidance, or help navigating services. A diagnosis will not be required.

Site staff and community partners will explain the program, distribute accessible information, and identify referral pathways. Give Miami Days will ask caregivers and children about language, schedule, transportation, accessibility, and preferred ways to participate. Families will help set goals and choose among components; they may decline a service or withdraw without penalty. Materials will explain program boundaries, confidentiality limits, and how to request a referral.

The coordinator will hold regular check-ins with site contacts and caregivers, collect child and family feedback, and review participation by site and age group. The project will use partner locations and schedules familiar to families and provide translated or interpreted communication when available. Where transportation or childcare support is feasible within the approved budget, staff will use it to reduce access barriers. Outreach and delivery changes will be made with family and partner input rather than assuming one model fits every neighborhood.

## 4. Assessment

The pilot aims to improve children’s connection to a trusted adult, strengthen caregiver awareness of support options, and make it easier for families to navigate school and community resources. At enrollment, the coordinator will record service goals, access needs, and baseline measures selected with partner input. At closeout, children and caregivers will be invited to complete the same brief measures. Attendance, mentor contacts, workshop participation, caregiver sessions, referral requests, and reported connection steps will be tracked throughout the year.

The pilot targets approximately 120 children served, at least 90 completing their planned support pathway, and at least 70% of participating children improving on one selected measure of belonging, help-seeking, or social-emotional skills. At least 75% of caregiver respondents should report increased awareness of support options. For families requesting referral navigation, the team will record whether a connection step occurred when consent permits. A coordinator will review data monthly and convene site partners quarterly to identify gaps or adapt delivery. Results will be disaggregated only when privacy can be protected and reported in aggregate. The year-end report will describe results and limitations; the pilot is not designed to establish causal clinical effects.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Planned pathway completion:** At least 90 of approximately 120 children complete the components selected in their support plan. Measure through attendance and service logs.
2. **Child progress:** At least 70% of participating children improve on one selected measure. Measure with the same age-appropriate baseline and closeout tool.
3. **Caregiver resource awareness:** At least 75% of caregiver respondents report increased awareness of support options. Measure through a closeout survey and report response rate.

## 5. Qualifications & Sustainability

Give Miami Days will assign a project lead to coordinate four sites, enrollment, mentor preparation, family communication, referrals, data collection, and reporting. Site partners will help identify participant needs and align activities with school and community procedures. Qualified professionals will review workshop content and provide or receive referrals within their scope. Before launch, the organization will document staff and mentor screening, training, safeguarding, consent, privacy, insurance, and partner responsibilities.

## 6. Advancing Equity

The integrated model is intended to reduce the burden on families of finding and coordinating multiple sources of support. Participation will not require a diagnosis or ability to pay. Families will be referred through trusted schools and community sites, invited to select goals, and asked about language, scheduling, transportation, disability access, and preferred forms of engagement. Children and caregivers will be able to raise concerns, request a mentor change, or decline a component without losing access to other available supports.

Give Miami Days will use plain-language communication, familiar locations, and translated or interpreted materials where qualified resources are available. The coordinator will review who enrolls, completes activities, receives referrals, and reports the program as accessible, while protecting privacy. If differences appear by site, age, language, or voluntarily shared demographic information, the team will consult families and partners to identify barriers and make adjustments. The organization will define priority communities with local input and avoid assuming that all families face the same barriers. Any support beyond the program’s capacity will be referred to qualified providers rather than handled informally.
