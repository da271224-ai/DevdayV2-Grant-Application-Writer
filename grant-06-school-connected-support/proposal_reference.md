## 1. Statement of Goals

Tidepath Family Alliance will connect 80 children and their caregivers to consistent mentors, family counseling or support from qualified providers, and school-based safety and mental-health workshops. In 12 months, at least 64 children will complete 24 mentoring contacts, and at least 70% of participating children will improve on one selected measure of belonging, coping, or help-seeking. Families will receive clear pathways to further support.

## 2. Implementation

The School-Connected Mentoring and Family Support program will coordinate three services around each child: a consistent mentor, caregiver support or counseling delivered by qualified providers, and age-appropriate safety and mental-health workshops at partner schools. The program will serve 80 children and their caregivers through four Miami-Dade school or community sites over 12 months. Mentoring and workshops are supportive and educational; clinical counseling will be provided only by qualified professionals.

**Months 1–2: Confirm partners and protections.** Secure site agreements, define referral and enrollment criteria, designate school and provider contacts, and document consent, privacy, child-safeguarding, and urgent-concern procedures. Confirm clinical provider qualifications, service capacity, fees, and referral arrangements. Recruit and screen mentors before matching.

**Months 2–3: Enroll and plan.** Enroll up to 80 children through partner referrals and family outreach. Obtain caregiver consent and child assent. Ask each family to identify goals, language and accessibility preferences, and schedule needs. Assign a coordinator as the family’s main program contact and determine which services the family wants.

**Months 3–10: Deliver coordinated supports.** Offer weekly mentor contact, targeting at least 24 contacts per completing child during the active program calendar. Provide family support sessions or counseling through qualified providers based on family choice and available capacity. Deliver at least four workshops per site on personal and emotional safety, recognizing stress, self-regulation, trusted adults, and how to seek help. The coordinator will check in with caregivers monthly and assist with referrals, with consent.

**Months 6 and 11–12: Review and close.** Review attendance, family feedback, and referral barriers with each site at midyear. Adapt schedules and outreach where feasible. At closeout, repeat participant measures, gather feedback, reconcile expenses, and prepare an aggregate report. Any urgent safety concern will follow partner protocols and applicable law.

### Activities Measures

1. **Mentoring:** Provide weekly contacts to 80 enrolled children; target at least 64 completing 24 contacts.
2. **Family support:** Offer qualified-provider support to families requesting it; target at least 50 families completing two or more sessions, subject to capacity and consent.
3. **School workshops:** Deliver at least four workshops at each of four partner sites and record student attendance and post-session learning checks.

## 3. Outreach & Engagement

The program will serve up to 80 children attending four participating Miami-Dade school or community sites and the caregivers responsible for their support. Referral partners may include school staff, family-serving organizations, or qualified providers. Enrollment will be voluntary and will not require a diagnosis or ability to pay. Caregivers will provide consent and children will be invited to assent in age-appropriate language.

Tidepath Family Alliance will explain the three service components, who provides each, privacy limits, and how to raise concerns. Families will be invited through trusted partner channels, family meetings, and accessible materials. The coordinator will ask about preferred language, schedule, transportation, disability access, and whether the family wants mentoring, counseling/support, workshops, or a combination. Families can decline any component without losing access to others.

Workshops will be held at familiar partner sites, with family sessions scheduled around caregiver availability where feasible. Translated communication and interpretation will be arranged when qualified resources are available. The coordinator will check in monthly to learn whether services remain useful and accessible. Attendance and feedback will be reviewed by site and age group while protecting identity. The program will adjust outreach or delivery with family and partner input when participation barriers appear.

## 4. Assessment

The program aims to improve children’s connection to a trusted adult, increase caregiver access to supportive services, and strengthen children’s knowledge of safety and help-seeking. At intake, each family will identify a goal and complete a short, age-appropriate baseline measure selected with partner input. At closeout, participants will be invited to complete the same measure. Staff will track mentor contacts, provider sessions, workshop attendance, family engagement, and referrals requested and completed when follow-up is permitted.

Targets are to enroll 80 children, have at least 64 complete 24 mentoring contacts, and have at least 70% of children with matched baseline and closeout data improve on one selected measure of belonging, coping, or help-seeking. At least 75% of caregiver respondents should report that they know how to request additional support. The coordinator will review participation monthly and discuss access or safety concerns with partners quarterly. Results will be reported in aggregate with response rates; counseling data will remain with providers except for information necessary and permitted for coordination. The program will describe observed change without claiming causal clinical effects.

### Outcomes Measures

1. **Mentoring completion:** At least 64 of 80 children complete 24 contacts. Measure through dated mentor logs, reviewed monthly.
2. **Child progress:** At least 70% of children with matched data improve on one selected measure. Measure with the same age-appropriate baseline and closeout tool.
3. **Caregiver support access:** At least 75% of caregiver respondents know how to request additional support. Measure through a closeout survey; report response rate.

## 6. Advancing Equity

Families may face cost, language, transportation, scheduling, disability-access, or information barriers when seeking mentoring and mental-health support. The program will be free to participants, will not require a diagnosis, and will let families select which components they want. Intake will ask caregivers and children about their own goals and access needs instead of assuming what support they need. Familiar school and community locations will reduce the need to navigate unfamiliar service systems.

Tidepath Family Alliance will use plain-language materials and arrange translation or interpretation where qualified resources are available. Families may request a different mentor or decline counseling without losing workshop access. Staff will explain confidentiality and safety limits before participation. Enrollment, completion, workshop feedback, and referral access will be reviewed by site and voluntarily provided demographic or access information when privacy can be protected. Partners and families will be consulted if some groups are less likely to enroll or complete services. The organization will identify priority communities with local input and adapt delivery to documented barriers.
